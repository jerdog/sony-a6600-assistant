# Lens-Category Shooting Guide (Sony a6600 / APS-C)

Reference by category. Effective focal length = stated length x 1.5.

## Fast primes (e.g. 35mm f/1.8, 50mm f/1.8, 85mm f/1.8)

- Effective range typically 50–130mm — portrait/general-purpose territory.
- Shoot 1–2 stops down from wide open (e.g. f/2.5–f/4 on an f/1.8 lens) for
  reliable sharpness on the subject; wide open is fine for eyes-in-focus
  portraits where background blur is the point.
- Use Eye AF + Single-point or Zone AF for portraits; Wide AF tends to grab
  the wrong plane on a shallow-DOF prime.
- Handheld floor: with IBIS alone (most primes have no OSS), aim for
  shutter speed ≥ 1/(effective focal length) as a starting point, e.g.
  1/80s minimum for a 50mm.

## Standard zooms (e.g. 16-55mm f/2.8, 18-135mm)

- Effective range ~24–200mm — the "one lens for the day" option.
- For general use, f/4–f/5.6 balances sharpness and enough light; open up
  to the lens's max aperture in low light rather than pushing ISO past
  ~6400 first.
- Wide AF or Zone AF works well since subjects are often more dynamic.

## Telephoto / wildlife (e.g. 70-350mm, 100-400mm)

- Effective range ~105–600mm+.
- Shutter speed matters more than aperture for freezing motion — for
  birds/wildlife, 1/1000s+ is a safer floor than the handheld-focal-length
  rule.
- Continuous AF (AF-C) + Zone or Tracking AF, drive mode Hi/Hi+ for
  action. Real-time Tracking is strong on the a6600 for erratic subjects.
- Electronic shutter can help hit high fps but watch for rolling-shutter
  distortion on fast-moving subjects (wings, vehicles).

## Macro (e.g. 30mm macro, 90mm macro)

- Effective range ~45–135mm depending on the lens; working distance
  matters more than focal length for skittish subjects (insects) —
  longer macro lenses give more room.
- Stop down more than usual (f/8–f/11) since depth of field at macro
  distances is extremely shallow even at moderate apertures.
- AF can hunt at close range — manual focus + focus peaking is often more
  reliable than AF-C for true macro work.
- A tripod or focus stacking helps more here than IBIS does.

## Wide-angle / astro (e.g. 10-18mm, 11mm f/1.8)

- Effective range ~15–27mm.
- For astro/Milky Way: widest aperture available, ISO 1600–3200 as a
  starting point, shutter speed via the "500 rule" adjusted for crop:
  500 / effective focal length ≈ max shutter before star trails
  (e.g. an 11mm lens, effective ~16.5mm → ~30s).
- Manual focus, zoomed-in live view on a bright star to nail infinity
  focus — AF is unreliable in the dark.
- IBIS should generally be turned OFF for long astro exposures on a
  tripod; it can introduce blur when there's no actual shake to correct.

## Video-specific notes (any lens)

- Watch focus breathing on cheaper primes/zooms — the a6600 doesn't have
  breathing compensation, so a lens that breathes will visibly shift
  framing when pulling focus.
- OSS lenses generally give smoother handheld video than IBIS alone;
  combine both when available ("Active" SteadyShot mode) for the
  steadiest handheld footage.
- For 4K, keep an eye on rolling shutter with fast pans on telephoto
  lenses in particular.
